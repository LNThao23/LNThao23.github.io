Dearly

Dearly is a voice-first mobile application designed to help older adults communicate with their loved ones and manage essential daily routines more easily. The app allows users to call family members by voice and receive simple medication reminders, while family members can remotely manage settings and receive lightweight activity updates.

Research Overview

Part 01 — Market Landscape

The Touch-First Usability Crisis

Most mobile applications are designed around visual-first and touch-first interactions, requiring users to recognize icons, navigate menus, and perform precise gestures.
For older adults aged 65+, these interactions become critical barriers.

visibility_off

Visual Limitations

Difficulty reading small text, low contrast indicators, and recognizing abstract flat icons.

front_hand

Motor & Dexterity

Tremors and reduced fine motor control make precise tapping, swiping, and pinch gestures unreliable.

account_tree

Complex Navigation

Losing place in nested submenus, ambiguous modal dismissals, and invisible back-button stacks.

report_problem

Fear of Errors

Constant fear of breaking device settings or deleting data, inducing reliance on children for basic tasks.

lightbulb

The Core Opportunity: A voice-first paradigm where voice acts as the direct interaction channel, while the screen serves solely as calm, high-contrast visual reassurance.

Part 02 — User Research

Qualitative Findings Across Two Generations

check_circle

Essential Usage Patterns: Older adults mainly use familiar functions such as calling and simple messaging.

check_circle

Dependent Setup: They often depend on their children to set up and explain unfamiliar phone features.

check_circle

Contact Retrieval Friction: Finding a contact through long alphabetical menus is frustrating and time-consuming.

check_circle

Risk Aversion: They strongly favor interactions that eliminate accidental mis-taps or unintended commands.

check_circle

Frictionless Adherence: Medication reminders should be voiced clearly and require zero complex modal button pushes.

check_circle

Quiet Reassurance: Family members want peace of mind regarding their parents' safety without intrusive surveillance.

Key Product Insight

Older adults need technology that feels simple and safe — rather than technology with more features.

Session 02

USER PERSONA

User Persona 01 — Primary Persona

Nguyen Van An

Age:72
Occupation:Retired
Tech Level:Basic smartphone user

“I just want to say my the name and make the call. Searching through the phone takes too many steps.”

Goals

  • •Easily call his children when needed
  • •Remember his daily medication
  • •Use his phone independently without asking for help

Behaviors

  • •Mainly uses calling and messaging
  • •Prefers speaking over navigating complicated menus
  • •Often asks his children for help with unfamiliar features

Pain Points

  • •Small text and icons are difficult to see
  • •Easily taps the wrong button
  • •Gets confused by multiple menus and settings
  • •Afraid of making mistakes on his phone

Needs

  • •Simple voice commands
  • •Large, clear information
  • •Minimal interaction steps
  • •Familiar and respectful language
  • •Clear confirmation before making a call
User Persona 02 — Secondary Persona

Nguyen Minh Anh

Age:32
Occupation:Office Worker
Tech Level:Comfortable with tech

“I don’t need to monitor my parents every minute. I just want to know that they are doing okay.”

Goals

  • •Remember parents’ medication
  • •Easily stay connected with them
  • •Check on their well-being
  • •Help manage the app remotely

Behaviors

  • •Regularly calls and messages her parents
  • •Helps set up and troubleshoot technology
  • •Checks in when something seems unusual

Pain Points

  • •Cannot always be there
  • •Must remind parents often
  • •Worries when they seem inactive
  • •Tech support can be time-consuming

Needs

  • •Medication scheduling
  • •Lightweight activity summaries
  • •Gentle abnormal-activity alerts
  • •Easy contact management
